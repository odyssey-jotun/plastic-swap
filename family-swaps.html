By John Griffin

What my family swapped, and what we paid

Between August 2024 and September 2025, my family replaced six everyday plastic products. Here is each swap, what we bought and what it cost.

I started researching microplastics in 2024 by watching health experts and doctors explain the studies. What I learned was enough for me to start changing what we buy.

We started in the kitchen, where food touches plastic every day, and moved on from there. Everything below comes straight from our order history.

Each swap

Cutting board: plastic to bamboo

Every cut on a plastic board scrapes off tiny pieces of plastic. The bamboo board was the cheapest swap we made and the easiest to start with.

Food storage: plastic to glass

Glass holds leftovers just as well as plastic. The lids are still plastic, so leave the lid off when you heat food.

Spatula: plastic to stainless steel

Plastic and nylon spatulas wear down against hot pans. Stainless steel holds up to the heat.

Underwear: synthetic to cotton

Polyester and nylon are plastics, and clothes made from them shed tiny fibers. Cotton is a natural fiber.

Dishwasher detergent: pods to plastic-free tablets

Many dishwasher pods are wrapped in a film of polyvinyl alcohol, a plastic made to dissolve in water. Plain tablets have no film at all.

Toothbrush: plastic to bamboo and boar bristle

A standard toothbrush has a plastic handle and nylon bristles. Ours have bamboo handles and natural boar bristles.
